How to use this

  • Treat every line as a question, not an answer — the generator gives you the shape, you supply the specifics
  • You don't need all four elements to agree neatly. Contradiction between them is often where the character gets interesting
  • The secret doesn't have to appear on the page. Characters act differently when they're carrying one, even unstated
  • Regenerate individual elements as many times as you like — there's no "correct" combination

Why this generator doesn't write your character for you

Most backstory generators take a name and a genre and hand back a paragraph of AI-written prose: a finished little biography you could paste straight into your notes. It reads fine and it means nothing, because none of it came from you. The character doesn't feel like yours because they aren't.

This one works differently. It gives you four separate building blocks, a formative event, a core wound, a lie the character believes about themselves or the world, and a secret they're keeping, each pulled independently so the combination is genuinely unexpected. Then it stops. Writing the character from those four pieces is the part that actually matters, and it's the part no generator can do for you.

The four building blocks

Formative event is the thing that happened before your story starts, the one your character has organised their life around whether they realise it or not.

Core wound is the belief that event left behind, the quiet rule the character lives by that they'd probably deny having if you asked them directly.

The lie they believe is the wound's logical conclusion, the sentence the character would say if they were being honest with themselves, which they mostly aren't.

A secret is what the character is actively managing right now, in the present of your story, separate from their history. It's the thing that makes them tense in a specific scene for a reason the reader doesn't have yet.

Why contradiction is useful

Don't discard a combination just because the pieces don't obviously fit together. A wound about needing to be needed and a secret about wanting to disappear aren't a mismatch, they're a person. Real people are inconsistent under pressure. If a generated combination surprises you, that's usually the one worth keeping.

Antagonists get their own lie

Switch the role toggle to antagonist and the lie they believe changes register, from self-limiting to self-justifying. A convincing antagonist doesn't think of themselves as the villain. They think the rules are for other people, or that they're owed something, or that someone worse would do it if they didn't. Give them a lie they'd defend at a dinner party, and they stop being a plot device.
