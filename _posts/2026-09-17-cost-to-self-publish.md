Self-publishing a novel to a professional standard costs somewhere between $500 and $5,000, depending on how much of the work you outsource versus do yourself. The two things that move that number the most are editing and cover design, everything else is comparatively small.

## The Non-Negotiables

Three line items separate a book that reads and looks professional from one that doesn't: editing, cover design, and formatting. Skipping any of them shows, and readers notice.

### Editing

Editing is several costs, not one, and you don't necessarily need all of them from a professional.

**Developmental editing:** $500 to $3,000, structural and craft-level feedback on the whole manuscript. Worth reading [what a developmental editor does](/blog/developmental-editor/) before budgeting for one, since it isn't always the right first step.

**Copyediting:** $300 to $1,500, grammar, consistency, and continuity at the sentence level.

**Proofreading:** $200 to $600, the final technical pass before publication.

A thorough [self-edit](/blog/self-edit-novel/), followed by honest beta reader feedback, reduces how much professional editing you need, though it rarely eliminates the need for a proofread entirely. Readers can tell the difference between a manuscript that's been through a proper technical pass and one that hasn't.

### Cover Design

**Premade covers:** $75 to $400, a designer's existing template with your title and author name dropped in. A legitimate option if your genre has strong visual conventions a template can satisfy.

**Custom design:** $300 to $1,200, built specifically for your book, with more control over how it represents the story.

**Premium custom:** $800 to $2,500, top-tier illustration or photography work, generally the territory of authors with an established readership and a bigger marketing budget behind the launch.

### Formatting

**DIY with free tools:** $0, workable but time-consuming, and the results vary depending on the tool and your patience with it.

**Dedicated formatting software:** roughly $150 to $250 as a one-time cost.

**Professional formatting service:** $150 to $500 per book.

## The Smaller Costs

**ISBN.** Free through KDP if you're only publishing there. Buying your own runs $125 for a single ISBN or around $295 for a pack of ten from Bowker, worth it if you're planning multiple books or multiple retailers under your own imprint name.

**Proof copy.** $4 to $12 for a single printed copy to check before the book goes live. Worth doing every time, catching a formatting problem in a physical proof is far better than catching it after launch.

**Copyright registration.** Around $65 in the US, optional but recommended if you want the strongest legal protection for your work.

**Marketing and launch.** Anywhere from $0 to several thousand pounds. ARC copies, promotional pricing, paid ads, and author website costs all sit here, and this is the most optional line item on the list, though skipping it entirely tends to slow discovery down.

## Three Realistic Budgets

**Bare minimum ($500 to $800):** A solid proofread, a premade cover, free or low-cost formatting, a free KDP ISBN. Produces a competent, professional-looking book. Doesn't include developmental editing or a custom cover.

**Mid-range ($1,500 to $2,500):** Copyediting plus proofreading, a custom cover, proper formatting, a small marketing budget for launch. The realistic target for a first-time self-published author taking the process seriously.

**Full professional ($4,000 and up):** Developmental editing plus copyediting plus proofreading, premium custom cover art, professional formatting, a real marketing and ARC campaign. The territory of authors treating this as a genuine business, often with a series or backlist already in progress.

## Where to Spend, Where to Save

**Spend on editing and the cover.** These are the two things readers judge before they've read a single sentence of your actual prose, the cover decides whether they click, and editing decides whether they finish and leave a review.

**Save on formatting.** This is the line item most easily reduced to zero without any quality loss, if your writing tool exports cleanly to a professional standard, there's no reason to pay for a separate formatting pass.

**Save on ISBNs if you're KDP-only.** The free KDP ISBN is fine unless you specifically need to publish under your own imprint or distribute beyond Amazon.

**Be honest about marketing.** A bigger marketing budget doesn't fix a book that wasn't ready to publish. Get the non-negotiables right first, editing, cover, formatting, then decide what's left for launch spend.

## Final Words

The real range is wide because the real question is how much of this work you're doing yourself versus paying someone else to do, not a single figure for "how much does self-publishing cost." Editing and cover design are worth genuine investment. Formatting, if your tools already handle it, doesn't have to be.
