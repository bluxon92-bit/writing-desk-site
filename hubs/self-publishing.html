How to self-publish a book, step by step

We take a closer look at how authors can take their work from a finished manuscript to your first thirty days on sale. Plus, we've included a free interactive self-publishing checklist so you can track progress as you go.

Overview The five phases Go deeper Get the checklist Format it in Writing Desk

Self-publishing a book means you need to take on every piece of the process that a traditional publisher would otherwise handle; editing, cover design, formatting, distribution, marketing, it all sits with you. This may sound overwhelming, however, the good news is that none of these steps are complicated in isolation. If you break it down and handle it step by step then it's very achievable to do well (I mean, come on, you've just written a novel - you got this).

That said, the part that trips people up is the sequencing. If you recruit ARC readers too late you won't have time to react to their feedback, if you do the cover design too late you may end up settling with something sub-par, and if you write the book description in the hour before hitting publish you will end up with something sub-par.

This guide lays the process out in five phases, starting eight weeks before your planned publication date, with a checklist available so you can tick items off as you go.

The five phases

Before you think about publishing, you first need a publish ready novel, polished to the nth degree. Once you have that the excitement starts.

  1. 1

    Foundations (8–6 weeks before)

    The foundations come first. This means a manuscript that's fully edited and proofread, not "mostly done", but fully polished. You also need to research the market, understand what's selling and brief a designer for a cover (briefed against what's selling in your category currently).

    This is also the phase where you need to think deeply about your books categorisation, comparable books in the market, the keywords you need to use, and your book description. Essentially, what language and images are you going to use to sell your book?

    • How to Self-Edit Your Novel — the manuscript needs to be finished before this phase starts
    • Designing Your Book Cover for Self-Publishing — the thumbnail test and genre conventions that matter
  2. 2

    Production (6–4 weeks before)

    This stage is where the manuscript becomes an actual product: formatted for print and ebook, front and back matter finalised, a proof copy ordered and reviewed. Your publishing platform account set up and verified, tax information and identity checks included, since these can take days to clear.

    Quick tip: You can professionally typeset and format your book for free using Writing Desks online formatter.

    • How to Self-Publish on Amazon KDP — account setup, file requirements, and the upload process itself
  3. 3

    Reader pipeline (4–2 weeks before)

    This is possibly the most-skipped phase because it can be irritating to find and get real feedback from ARC readers. But it is important you don't get tempted to jump ahead.

    ARC readers need to be recruited with enough runway to finish the book and write something thoughtful before you publish. The aim here is to launch with reviews already under your belt, and you may also catch some essential feedback, either about your cover, book description, or even about the manuscript itself that needs fixing pre-launch.

    • The Self-Publishing Checklist — the full reader-pipeline checkpoints, tickable
  4. 4

    Launch week

    The book goes live, the launch email you already wrote gets sent, ARC readers are asked to post the reviews they already have ready. Early sales and positive reviews are what gets a book noticed by retailer algorithms in the first place, so this week carries real weight.

  5. 5

    Post-launch (first 30 days)

    Publishing is the first milestone, not the end of the process. You need to track your sales and rankings, monitor rand respond to reviews. And you need to keep posting about your story, sharing updates with your audience, and decide whether or not to commit some budget into a promotional push.

    A countdown deal or a temporary price drop may also be worth running to give the book a second wave of visibility.

The real lever

Launch day results are decided weeks before launch day.

By the time the book goes live, the manuscript, the cover, and the formatting are already locked. What's still moveable that week is entirely about the reader pipeline built in the weeks before it, ARC reviews ready to post, an audience that already knows the date.

Sequencing the five phases in order is what makes that pipeline exist by the time it's needed.
